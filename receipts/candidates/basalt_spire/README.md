# basalt_spire candidates

- **a, primitives:** stacks of six-sided cylinder drums whose taper leaves a ledge at each joint, sheared glassy caps, box streaks, squat hexagonal masses at the root. `ok basalt_spire_a 1248 tris 55 meshes 4.105x9x4.127m`
- **b, profiles (picked):** six-segment lathes whose profile carries a flared fused root, V-notched joints, a taper and a sheared glassy break, twisted slowly about their own axis; extruded flame-shaped lilac streaks on the three sky-facing faces; a lumpy lathed lava mound. `ok basalt_spire_b 1816 tris 26 meshes 4.234x9x4.381m`
- **c, honeycomb:** hand-built prisms packed edge to edge so they touch at the root, three tall cells sheared into their lean and twisted, the rest broken off low, lilac ribbons laid on the twisted faces. `ok basalt_spire_c 572 tris 31 meshes 4.53x9x4.028m`

B reads as volcanic first and alien second: the notched joints make columnar basalt legible at 20 m, the twist and the purple-black with lilac veins put it plainly off this world, and the mound fuses the three roots. A's joints vanish at distance and its streaks came out as a row of lozenges; c reads as smooth obsidian crystal rather than basalt.

Known weaknesses: night basalt is nearly black, so the spire needs a lighter backdrop or a rim light to separate; the streaks sit on the uphill faces, so from some angles only one column shows lilac; the lean makes the bounding box 4.2 x 4.4 m, slightly over the 4 m base. The glassy breaks are a second material (same colour, roughness 0.28).

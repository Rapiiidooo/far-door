# expedition_rope candidates

All three share one anchor layout, so the comparison is about construction: the rope lies on the lip from a 0.22 m aged-bronze ring (hanging 55° towards the edge from the eye of a piton leaning back 25°), turns over the corner and hangs in front of the face in a gentle S to a thick knot, lowest point at y = 0. `userData.anchor` is the lip corner under the rope after the placement shift (the lip top is the plane y = anchor[1], the face the plane z = anchor[2]); `userData.mounts = 'back'`. Every part's transform is baked into its geometry and merged per material, so the loader's re-origin is zero and the anchor holds whether the asset is loaded merged or with `keepHierarchy`.

- A, primitives: cylinders and torus arcs (a quarter torus over the corner, three arcs of alternating hand for the S), two torus wraps on the ring, a monkey's fist of a sphere and three tori, torus ring and eye, a square tapered piton, a four-sided tapered wedge. `ok  expedition_rope_a  1832 tris  3 meshes  0.354x2.028x0.423m  [flat by declaration: back]`
- B, sweeps: one TubeGeometry along one centreline sampled at its control points (dense in the round turn and the knot), a hand-placed overhand knot, lathed flare-and-fibre ends, lathed ring, eye and piton, a bevelled extruded wedge. `ok  expedition_rope_b  1912 tris  3 meshes  0.361x2.038x0.455m  [flat by declaration: back]`
- C, second reading, hand-built lofts: the rope doubled the way climbers rig a ring to abseil, its middle passed through the ring, both legs over the lip side by side, twisting round each other down the S and tied together in one thick doubled overhand knot with two frayed tails; one loft on rotation-minimising frames. `ok  expedition_rope_c  1984 tris  3 meshes  0.336x2.008x0.439m  [flat by declaration: back]`

Picked C. Hung by its anchor from a 3 m sandstone ledge in the game's rig (`scratch/rig_lit_*.png`, `scratch/rig_shade_0.png`, `scratch/rig_side_*.png`), it is the boldest line at 9 and 12 m: two twisting legs about 10 cm wide hold up where A and B thin to a 3 px thread, and it reads as rigged rope rather than a cord. B is the literal single rope, with the best overhand knot, if the doubled reading is not wanted. A reads as a smooth cord.

Two things changed on the way. A laid three-strand twist looked sawn at the ring spacing 2,000 triangles allow, so the ropes are smooth and the fabric surface supplies the fibre. The first 0.18 m ring was a 7 px dot at 9 m, so the ring grew to 0.22 m and hangs steeper.

Known weaknesses: 1,984 of 2,000 triangles, so no headroom; the piton stands about 0.27 m proud of the lip, taller than a real one, so the ring reads at 9 to 12 m; the rope khaki cools to grey-green on a shaded face; the fibres vanish beyond about 5 m; the anchor assumes a flat top and a vertical face, so a rough cliff skin can bury or lift the legs by a few centimetres. On a face turned by a yaw, rotate the anchor with the asset: `position = corner - R(yaw) * anchor`.
